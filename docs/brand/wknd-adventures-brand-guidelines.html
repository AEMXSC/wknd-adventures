01 · Foundation

Brand at a Glance

WKND Adventures is an editorial outdoor publication that documents the people, routes, and moments that make the outdoors worth protecting. This guide condenses positioning, editorial guardrails, voice, and the core visual system into one reference.

Bold Stories. Real Life. Wild Places.

We write for the reader who is three weeks out and needs to know exactly what they're getting into. Every route is walked before it's written; every report carries a last-verified date. The tone is honest and field-tested — never brochure fluff, never gatekeeping.

Design Principles
  • Honest over hyped — we publish the hard days, not just the summits: the bad weather, the route that didn't go, the gear that failed.
  • Field-first, not desk research — specifics come from people who actually went.
  • For every level, no gatekeeping — truthful difficulty ratings, welcoming to first-timers and alpinists alike.
Key Characteristics
  • Photography-forward layouts built around full-bleed, dawn-and-dusk wilderness imagery.
  • Wide display type meets clean sans body: Syncopate for headlines, Instrument Sans for reading.
  • A trail-and-dusk palette — warm amber energy over deep pine, grounded in warm neutrals.
  • Signature hard-offset "trail-sign" buttons and generously rounded cards.

02 · Strategy

Market Positioning

WKND Adventures is a first-hand adventure publication. It replaces endless research and secondhand trip reports with verified, walked-it-ourselves guides, gear, and field journals you can actually plan around.

Positioning Statement

For serious outdoor people who are tired of aggregated, unverified beta, WKND Adventures is the first-hand publication that replaces guesswork with ground truth — real routes, real conditions, and real gear, documented in the field and updated when the trail changes. Unlike hype-driven wanderlust feeds, we publish only what we've confirmed ourselves.

Audience
  • Serious outdoor people planning multi-day routes who need exact, current beta.
  • Seasoned alpinists seeking detailed, honest accounts of routes they haven't done.
  • Independent adventurers who plan their own trips rather than outsource them.
Differentiators
  • First-hand, not aggregated — every route completed before publication.
  • Verified & dated — each report carries a last-verified date.
  • Updated, not abandoned — we revisit when conditions shift.
  • Honest difficulty — truthful ratings, never gatekeeping.

03 · Compliance

Editorial Guardrails

Because WKND Adventures sends people into the real world — trails, water, weather, and remote places — every claim must protect the reader, the outdoors, and the publication's credibility. These guardrails are mandatory across all channels.

Non-Negotiable Rules
  • Walk it before you write it. No route, camp, or crossing is published without a contributor completing it first.
  • Date every claim. Conditions change — carry a last-verified date and revisit when the trail materially shifts. We treat outdated advice on active terrain as negligence.
  • Name the risk, skill, and conditions. Encourage proper gear, training, and guides. Never frame danger as a challenge to prove.
  • Respect nature — Leave No Trace. Promote low-impact travel. Never encourage off-trail damage, wildlife disturbance, or littering.
  • Be honest about difficulty. Rate routes truthfully and inclusively; state the fitness, permits, and experience they require.
  • Respect people and place. Honor local cultures, lands, and regulations, and ask readers to do the same.

04 · Voice

Brand Voice

WKND Adventures sounds like the contributor who already walked the route — honest, prepared, and generous with the details. It respects the reader's intelligence and their safety in equal measure.

Voice Attributes
  • Honest & first-hand — specifics from experience, never brochure fluff.
  • Prepared & practical — gear, timing, difficulty, and the next step.
  • Welcoming, not gatekeeping — you belong out here, whatever your level.
  • Understated, not hyped — the place is the drama; we don't manufacture it.
  • Responsible — respects nature, safety, and local communities.
Style Rules
  • Write for the reader who is three weeks out from the trip.
  • Be specific: name the route, the season, the camp, the water source.
  • Break trips into gear lists, day-by-day, and conditions — never a wall of text.
  • Lead with the honest assessment, then the practical detail.
Tone by Context

Guides: exact, practical, verified. Field Notes: reflective, personal, vivid. Community: open, inclusive, celebratory.

Voice in one line

"We go first, we document everything, and we update our work — so the outdoors you read about is the outdoors you'll actually find."
